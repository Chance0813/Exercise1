General Info

Italy, a European country with a long Mediterranean coastline, has left a powerful mark on Western culture and cuisine. Its capital, Rome, is home to the Vatican as well as landmark art and ancient ruins. Other major cities include Florence, with Renaissance masterpieces such as Michelangelo’s "David" and Brunelleschi's Duomo; Venice, the city of canals; and Milan, Italy’s fashion capital. U.S. citizens can travel to Italy for up to 90 days without a visa, but your passport must be valid for at least three to six months beyond your departure date.

The top things to do in Italy include exploring ancient Roman ruins in Rome, riding a gondola in Venice, and viewing Renaissance art in Florence. Step back in time at the Colosseum: Walk through the massive ancient arena in Rome where gladiators once fought. Glide through Venice: Take a traditional boat ride along the historic canals and under the bridges of Venice.

Venice

Venice, Italy is one of my favorites places I have ever visited outside of the U.S. I went there for the first time with my girlfriend and we rode on a gondola, ate lots of pasta and learned about the history of Venice. Venice is a famous romantic coastal city in northeastern Italy built across more than 100 small islands in a lagoon. One thing we learned while we were here is that Venice is essentially a man made island built on top of lumber.

The best things to do in Venice include exploring iconic landmarks like Saint Mark's Basilica and the Doge's Palace, riding a gondola, and wandering through the historic neighborhoods. Do a Cicchetti Crawl: Stop at traditional local wine bars called bacari in the Cannaregio or Dorsoduro districts to sip a Venetian spritzer and eat small finger foods (cicchetti). Libreria Acqua Alta: Browse this quirky, famous canal-side bookstore where books are piled inside old gondolas and bathtubs.
